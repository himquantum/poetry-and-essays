The Paradox of All Knowledge

A poem by himquantum
We built a library with infinite shelves and then we built walls inside it. The algorithm learned our taste the way a mirror learns your face, by reflecting it endlessly until you forget there's a world behind the glass. A click away: every fact ever documented, every perspective ever written, the collected wisdom of millennia sitting in your pocket like a gem you refuse to touch. Instead you adore the same pebble daily, polished smooth by repetition, comforted by its familiar weight. I watch people hold the world in their hands and choose to see only their corner of it. The search bar glows with infinite possibility and they don't dare to type a few words. This is the cruelest trick the information age ever played: it gave us a planet and we chose the smallest cave. We called it freedom. We built algorithmic prisons with open doors and never noticed we locked ourselves in. Confirmation bias is not stupidity. It is the mind's way of saying: I am afraid of being wrong. Let me rest in the familiar. Let me surround myself with voices that sing my song back to me. The echo chamber does not silence the world. It just makes your own voice so loud you cannot hear anything else. And the tragedy is this: We have never been more informed. We have never been more certain. We have never understood less. The paradox is not that information exists. The paradox is that abundance has become a prison. We are drowning in data and parched for truth. We mistake popularity for validity. We mistake the loudness of our bubble for the sound of reality. A familiar actor says something about history, A familiar player teaches biology, and we call it proof. not realizing we've only travelled in circles. The world is wider than your feed. Truth is stranger than your algorithm. Reality refuses to be personalized. But you will not know this until you leave the room. Until you unmute the voices that make you uncomfortable. Until you search for what you're afraid to find. And most of us will not. Most of us will die in the cave we chose surrounded by mirrors, convincing ourselves we are looking out the window.
